The Adventures of Mooney & Raichu · Episode 2 · Shooting script

House Rules

Every attempt to get rid of him improves the house.

Gary moves in properly, as promised, and Lyndie compliments the house for the first time in nine years. Mooney and Raichu declare war. Every trap they set fails on its own and leaves the place tidier than it found it, Gary never once notices he is under attack, and by the end the humans are right to prefer him.

Production notes

Read the bible before writing a prompt

series/EPISODE-BIBLE.md carries the whole craft: prompt anatomy in order, every failure mode this show has hit with the exact wording that fixes it, the audio direction, the edit spec, the real costs and the content filter's behaviour. These notes cover what is specific to this episode; the bible covers what is true of all of them. prompts/blocks.py is the bible made executable — import it and the rules are applied whether you remember them or not.

The edit has a spec now, not just a loudness pass

From the owner's full-episode document, 2026-09-13. Its editing master prompt asks for things assemble.py has never done: J-cuts and L-cuts so audio carries across a cut; reaction shots kept after punchlines; no cutting immediately after every spoken sentence; 0.5–1.5s of silence around important jokes; 2–3 frame impact accents on major crashes; consistent screen direction; and the summary line worth pinning above the edit bay — "the finished cartoon must feel intentionally storyboarded rather than a collection of unrelated AI video clips." The TRIM/TAIL tables get us the reaction holds and the silence; the J-cuts and the impact accents are new work and both are free in ffmpeg.

The 6–10 dB duck has to come from the prompt, not the mix

That document specifies the score ducking 6 to 10 dB under dialogue and returning between lines, which is exactly right and now in AUDIO_MIX. It cannot be done in our assembler: Seedance hands back one pre-mixed track per shot with score, Foley and dialogue baked together, and there are no stems to duck. Asking for it in the prompt is the only lever we have, so it matters that it is asked for in every one.

Do not fill every second

The one instruction most likely to improve the finished thing: "do not fill every second with music or effects — silence and reaction pauses are part of the comedy." Our per-act score design risks exactly that failure, so the sentence is now in every prompt in capitals. Episode 1's B-9 and B-11 — six blank cat faces in total silence, held far too long — are the proof it works; they are the best-timed shots in the episode.

Humans hear meows. It is canon now

Also from that document: humans cannot understand the cats; whenever they speak in front of a human, the human hears ordinary meowing. House Rules Act Four is built on it — Michael and Lyndie photograph Gary while both cats stand there dripping and complaining, and none of it lands unless the humans genuinely cannot hear them. MEOWS goes on every shot with a person and a talking animal in the same frame.

The episode has a score, designed once

Episode 1 had none: each shot named an instrument and the finished cut had no through-line. prompts/blocks.py now holds one cue state per act, named by what the music is doing, and audio() stamps the right one onto every prompt automatically — cold open light playful pizzicato, unsuspecting; Act One a small self-important bureaucratic march; Act Two scheming tiptoe pizzicato building into a full Tom-and-Jerry chase orchestra; Act Three low noir bass, a deal being done in the dark; Act Four warm domestic music entirely on Gary's side, which is the joke; resolution fully earnest strings. Transitions — "…music begins", "the music stops dead" — go in the shot where they happen.

Direct the mix, not just the ingredients

From the owner's example prompt of 2026-09-13. Episode 1 ended every prompt with a list — "wind, one distant gull, crickets." — and left the balance to chance. Every prompt now ends with the mix itself: clear centered dialogue, subtle room ambience, cartoon Foley, precisely timed comedic SFX, original orchestral score that ducks under speech and stops dead on the punchline, no copyrighted music. "Clear centered dialogue" and "ducks under speech" go straight at the complaint that drove the whole Episode 1 rework.

Ask for the silence, not just the absence of words

The same prompt says "brief comedic silence after Mooney's line". Episode 1's cap only forbade extra speech, which left a vacuum the model filled anyway. only() now asks for the silence by name and tells the score to stop on the line. Better comedy and a tighter cap in one.

Time-code the beats in an action take

The example carries four gags in one fifteen-second generation by writing 0–4 sec:, 4–8 sec: and pinning the audio to each. beats() does that; C-4's montage is written this way. It buys reliability and rhythm rather than credits — cost is per second either way — but a trap and its payoff can no longer come back as two takes that fail to match.

"Harmless slapstick only"

Also lifted from that prompt, and Act Two is nothing but pratfalls. The filter refused two of fourteen trailer shots over wording that was in no way unsafe, so every trap shot carries nobody is hurt, nobody is injured, everyone is fine immediately afterwards. Free insurance on the most exposed act in the episode.

Write the sound effect into the action, in capitals, on the beat

Taken from the owner's Lucky Lots prompt (higgsfield.ai/s/kMh3mXz6BqY), which reads "WHOOSH, a second rocket launches upward … a split second before BOOM, an enormous colorful cartoon explosion". The onomatopoeia sits inline at the exact moment it lands, and the terse Audio: list still goes last. Every action line in this script is written that way already — CLUNK, SKKKRRT, SPROING, FWOOMPH — so it transfers into the prompt verbatim. Unverified: that sample was generated on Seedance 2.5, so its sound design may owe as much to the model as to the wording. Shoot one trap shot both ways before committing the act.

Also from that prompt: the negative, and the delivery

It carries "NO on-screen text, no subtitles, no captions, no logos, no watermarks" as one blunt line — stronger than this project's current wording, and worth adopting wholesale. It also puts the delivery next to the line rather than in a separate voice block: says warmly in a strong New York accent: "…". Same shape this project already uses for Mooney and Raichu.

Stay on Seedance 2.0 unless a shot needs the length

Priced 2026-09-12: 2.0 is 4.5 credits a second, 2.5 is 6.5 (36 versus 52 for eight seconds). 2.5 goes to 30 seconds where 2.0 stops at 15, and the Lucky Lots prompt shows it holding a multi-beat sequence with an internal quick cut to close-up. That earns its 44% premium only where one long take replaces three shots that would each waste the four-second minimum — B-11's montage is the one candidate here. Everything else is cheaper on 2.0.

Every dialogue shot is single-speaker

Not one shot in this episode has two. Every line in the episode is recoverable for 1 credit. voice_change converts every voice on a track, so a shot where both cats speak can never be put on the locked voices — that is why Episode 1's CO-2 had to be re-shot. Mooney is Cillian, Raichu is Miles. Keep it that way and each line costs 1 credit to lock.

Cap every line in the prompt

Seedance pads a line under about six words with a second, invented utterance. Four of the owner's Episode 1 notes were this and nothing else. Use the only() wording from ep02-chonk/prompts/refix.py on every line here — this script has a lot of very short ones, which is exactly the failure case.

Silent shots are the reliable ones

Twenty-six of these sixty-three shots have no dialogue at all. That is deliberate: a shot with no speech cannot be padded, cannot be mumbled and never needs revoicing. It is also what Tom and Jerry actually is. State it explicitly — there is no dialogue in this shot, nobody speaks — or the model will add some.

Over-generate the action, then trim

Measured on the trailer: physical action lands somewhere unpredictable inside the shot, and both two-cat shots left dead frame at one end. Generate action a second or two long and cut IN and OUT per shot the way series/trailer/assemble.py does, rather than head-trimming only.

Lock every two-cat frame

B-9, C-4, C-5 and D-2 all have both cats moving at once, the hardest thing this pipeline does. Static locked-off wide, both cats named with their side of frame, and both cats remain fully inside the frame for the entire shot — and expect to trim an exit anyway.

Budget one refusal in seven

The content filter rejected two of fourteen trailer shots, twice each, over wording that was in no way unsafe. Slapstick is more exposed than dialogue, so for every trap shot decide the free fallback before generating: B-3 can be a still of the bucket, B-9 can hold on Gary, and the whole of B-11 can be built from the other traps' offcuts. Rejected jobs are not charged.

The music scene is one shot, not a sequence

C-4 asks for the montage inside a single 12-second generation, because cutting four separate 4-second minimums together would cost 72 credits for four seconds of usable footage. If it comes back weak, build the montage locally from the trap shots already in the can and score it with a synthesised cue — divider.py and outro.py already synthesise audio with numpy, for nothing.

Dividers and cards are free

Five dividers at the act breaks, from the four designs in ep02-chonk/divider.py, cycling so no two adjacent match. Main title and end plate are reused. The episode line and the teaser text composite in post with drawtext. None of it costs anything.

Narration and score go on afterwards

There is no narrator in this episode, but the same lesson holds for the music: ask Seedance for the sound design per shot in the Audio: line, then do the wide musical decisions in the assembler. seed_audio is 0.1 credits a line if anything needs speaking.

Gary needs a voice before a frame is shot

He carries five lines here against three in Episode 1, and he is the antagonist. Pick his preset from series/VOICES.html and write it into the handoff table first. Gerald needs one too — three lines. Assigning them up front is the whole lesson of Episode 1.

Cutaways still solve continuity

Five hard jumps to unrelated scenes — A-7, B-7, B-18, D-5, D-10 — where the discontinuity is the joke. Do not chain a reference frame into them. Everywhere else, pass the previous shot's final frame, and check the last frame of each shot against the first of the next.

Nothing readable in frame

The chore wheel, Raichu's plan on the cereal packet and Gary's crossword must all be scratches and marks, never letters. Ask for it explicitly on A-1, A-3, B-1, D-9 and every Gary shot. Real text composites in post.
